45 Years Later: 1981 Antwerp Synagogue Bombing Memorial

Antwerp pauses on Hoveniersstraat to honor the victims of the 1981 terror attack and confront the enduring reality of community security

Acting Mayor Els van Doesburg addresses dignitaries, community leaders, and residents in front of the Portuguese Synagogue on Hoveniersstraat during the 45th anniversary memorial, October 20, 2026.

Overview: On Tuesday, 20 October 2026, exactly forty-five years after a devastating truck bomb exploded outside the Portuguese Synagogue in Antwerp’s diamond district, the city and its Jewish community gathered on Hoveniersstraat for a solemn memorial ceremony. Organized by the Forum der Joodse Organisaties (FJO), the commemoration brought together roughly 150 participants — including municipal leadership, community representatives, survivors, and law enforcement — to honor the three victims killed, remember more than a hundred wounded, and reflect upon four and a half decades of vigilance, memory, and unwavering resilience.

A Solemn Gathering on Hoveniersstraat

The memorial was held directly outside the entrance of the historic Portuguese Synagogue (built in 1913), on the very stones where the massive explosion erupted on the morning of 20 October 1981. Worshippers, community elders, civic authorities, and local police officers stood side by side in the autumn morning air to pay tribute.

Wreaths and floral arrangements were laid before the synagogue entrance. A moment of silence was observed, followed by traditional memorial prayers and psalms recited in memory of the victims who lost their lives on that morning just moments before the Simchat Torah holiday prayers were to commence.

Voices of Remembrance and Vigilance

Prominent leaders addressed the gathered assembly, voicing both profound sorrow for the past and a clear-eyed assessment of present realities.

Regina Sluszny, president of the Forum der Joodse Organisaties (FJO) and herself a Holocaust survivor, spoke movingly about the enduring psychological and communal impact of the 1981 bombing. She emphasized that the passage of forty-five years has not diminished the sacred obligation to remember the victims and comfort those whose lives were forever altered by shrapnel, loss, and trauma.

Antwerp’s Acting Mayor, Els van Doesburg, delivered a poignant address from the city podium. She underlined the deep historical bond between the city of Antwerp and its Jewish inhabitants, while confronting the painful reality that forty-five years after the bombing, Jewish institutions, schools, and houses of worship throughout Antwerp still require extensive, around-the-clock security protection and fortified barriers.

"It remains deeply painful and sobering that forty-five years after this cowardly terrorist bombing, our Jewish fellow citizens still cannot walk to synagogue or send their children to school without extensive security measures and armed vigilance. We must never accept antisemitism as an inevitable fact of life."

Van Doesburg reiterated the city’s unyielding commitment to the safety and freedom of its Jewish community, calling for ongoing societal vigilance against all forms of antisemitism, intolerance, and extremist violence.

Remembering the 1981 Attack

The commemoration served as an important bridge to history for younger generations who did not witness the 1981 blast firsthand. On that morning, a delivery truck packed with powerful explosives had been left parked overnight directly in front of the synagogue under the pretext of a mechanical breakdown. Shortly after 9:00 AM, the vehicle detonated, blasting through the synagogue's reinforced wooden doors and ornate stained glass windows, shattering shopfronts across blocks of the diamond district, killing three people, and injuring 106 others.

For an in-depth account of the attack itself, the police investigations, and the international context of the era, read our detailed archive article: The 1981 Antwerp Synagogue Bombing.

45 Years of Resilience and Community

Despite the devastation wrought in 1981, Antwerp’s Jewish community chose not to retreat from public life or surrender the heart of the diamond quarter. In the four and a half decades since, the community has restored the Portuguese Synagogue, rebuilt surrounding businesses, and continued to be an integral part of Antwerp's economic and cultural life.

The 45th anniversary ceremony was a testament not only to sorrow and grief, but to the enduring strength, faith, and continuity of Jewish life in Antwerp.
