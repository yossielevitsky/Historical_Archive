Machsike Hadas

מחזיקי הדת

History

Established in the late 19th century, Machsike Hadas (meaning "Upholders of the Faith") is the principal ultra-Orthodox (Haredi) umbrella organization in Antwerp. It was founded to maintain strict traditional standards of Jewish law (Halakha) in areas such as kashrut (dietary laws), education, and religious services, serving various Hasidic and non-Hasidic Orthodox groups in the city.

World War II and Rebuilding

During World War II, the Machsike Hadas community was devastated by the Holocaust. Its Chief Rabbi, Marcus Rottenberg, was deported and murdered in Auschwitz. Following the war, survivors immediately set out to rebuild the community, restoring synagogues, schools, and kosher certification systems to ensure the continuation of traditional Jewish life in Antwerp.

Today

Today, Machsike Hadas represents one of the largest and most vibrant Haredi communities in Europe. It oversees a network of synagogues, schools, and social services, and is world-renowned for its strict kashrut supervision, making Antwerp a major center of Orthodox Jewish life.
