Red Star Line

The ocean passenger line that carried millions of emigrants to America.

Placeholder for Red Star Line image.

Overview: The Red Star Line was a shipping line founded in 1871 that operated ocean passenger ships between Antwerp, Philadelphia, and New York, serving as a gateway to America for millions of European emigrants, including many Jewish families seeking new lives.

Antwerp as a Transit Hub

From 1873 to 1934, the ocean liners of the Red Star Line transported more than two million passengers from Antwerp to America. For hundreds of thousands of Jewish emigrants fleeing pogroms, poverty, and rising antisemitism in Eastern Europe, Antwerp became the main gateway to a new life in the United States and Canada.

Emigrants arrived by train at Antwerp's Central Station, where they underwent medical examinations and disinfection before boarding the steamships. While waiting for their departure, many stayed in temporary lodging houses in the city's Jewish quarter. This temporary presence contributed to the growth of local Jewish infrastructure, as kosher food, synagogues, and support networks were established to assist the travelers.

Settling in Antwerp

Although the vast majority of Jewish travelers used Antwerp strictly as a transit point, a significant number chose to settle in the city permanently. These new residents brought with them skills, traditions, and energy that transformed Antwerp's Jewish community, particularly through their involvement in the diamond trade. Today, the historic Red Star Line warehouses in Antwerp serve as a museum, commemorating this mass migration and the stories of those who journeyed across the Atlantic.
