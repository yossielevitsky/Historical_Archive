Shomre Hadas

שומרי הדת

History

Shomre Hadas (meaning "Guardians of the Faith"), officially the Communauté Israélite de Shomre Hadas, is the main modern Orthodox congregation in Antwerp. It was established in the 19th century, representing mostly Dutch and German Jewish immigrants who settled in Antwerp. Its flagship synagogue, the Hollandse Synagoge (Dutch Synagogue) on Bouwmeestersstraat, was opened in 1893.

World War II and Rebuilding

During the German occupation, the congregation's synagogues were closed and damaged, and many members were deported. Following the war, Shomre Hadas played a key role in the reconstruction of Jewish life in Antwerp, reopening the Hollandse Synagoge in 1958 and the Romi Goldmuntz Synagogue, and re-establishing communal services for returning families.

Today

Today, Shomre Hadas is the largest officially recognized Jewish congregation in Antwerp, representing the Modern Orthodox and traditional segments of the community. It manages synagogues, cemeteries, and religious services, and is recognized by the Belgian state as a representative body for Antwerp's Jewish community.
