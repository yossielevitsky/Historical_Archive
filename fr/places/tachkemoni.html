Tachkemoni School

בית הספר תחכמוני

History

Founded in 1920, the Tachkemoni school was established in Antwerp under the banner of the Religious Zionist Mizrachi movement. Unlike the strictly traditional Jesode Hatorah, Tachkemoni integrated modern Hebrew language instruction and Zionist philosophy alongside traditional religious studies and a rigorous general curriculum. It quickly became the cultural hub for modern-oriented Orthodox families in Antwerp.

World War II and Rebuilding

The school's operations were tragically disrupted by the German invasion in 1940. During the occupation, its building was confiscated, and the community suffered devastating losses due to deportations. Shortly after liberation, in May 1945, the school reopened to welcome returning children, providing them with a supportive environment to heal and resume their education in post-war Antwerp.

Today

Today, Tachkemoni continues to serve as a modern Orthodox, co-educational day school. Fully recognized and subsidized by the Flemish government, it maintains its dual curriculum of Hebrew, Jewish history, and religious texts, combined with a strong secular education. It remains a vital institution representing the diversity of Antwerp's Jewish educational landscape.
