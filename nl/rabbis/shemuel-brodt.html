Rabbi Shemu'el Brodt

שמואל ברודט

Overview: Rabbi Shemu'el (Samuel) Brodt was a distinguished rabbi, Religious Zionist leader, and public figure who served as Chief Rabbi of Antwerp from 1936 to 1941. Before arriving in Belgium, he was active in both the Polish rabbinate and the Mizrachi movement, and he also served as a member of the Polish Parliament. Following his escape from Nazi-occupied Belgium during the Second World War, he continued his rabbinical career in the United States before later settling in Israel.

Early Life and Education

Shemu'el Brodt was born in Łódź, Poland, in 1885. After the death of his father during his childhood, he was raised by his grandfather, Rabbi Shlomo Plato, a follower of the Radzyner Hasidic dynasty.

He studied under Rabbi Meir Yechiel Halstock, the Ostrovtzer Rebbe, and developed a reputation as an accomplished Torah scholar. At the age of twenty-two, he received his first rabbinical appointment in the town of Brisk in Poland. He subsequently served as rabbi in several Polish communities.

Public Leadership in Poland

Alongside his rabbinical duties, Rabbi Brodt became active in the Mizrachi movement, eventually serving as its president in Poland. He also represented Mizrachi in the Polish Sejm (Parliament), where he advocated for Jewish communal interests and Religious Zionist ideals.

While serving as Rabbi of Tomaszów Mazowiecki, he worked to improve the position of the rabbinate by encouraging communities to provide fixed salaries for rabbis instead of relying on fees collected for religious services. He believed this would strengthen both the dignity and independence of the rabbinate.

Chief Rabbi of Antwerp

In 1936, Rabbi Brodt succeeded Rabbi Moshe Avigdor Amiel as Chief Rabbi of Antwerp, one of the largest Jewish communities in Western Europe.

He served the community during an increasingly difficult period as political tensions in Europe intensified. Following the German invasion and occupation of Belgium in 1940, conditions for the Jewish community rapidly deteriorated. Rabbi Brodt remained in Antwerp until 1941, when he succeeded in escaping occupied Belgium and emigrated to the United States.

Later Years

After arriving in New York, Rabbi Brodt became rabbi of the Moriah Congregation in Manhattan, where he served for approximately nine years.

He later emigrated to Israel and was appointed a judge on the Supreme Rabbinical Court (Beit Din HaGadol) under the Chief Rabbinate. In this role he continued to contribute to Jewish religious life and halachic scholarship until the end of his life.

Death and Legacy

Rabbi Shemu'el Brodt passed away on 8 Nisan 5723 (1963).

He is remembered as a respected Torah scholar, an important leader of the Religious Zionist movement in Poland, and a rabbi whose career spanned four countries—Poland, Belgium, the United States, and Israel. His years in Antwerp coincided with one of the most challenging periods in the history of the city's Jewish community, making him an important figure in the history of Belgian Jewry.
