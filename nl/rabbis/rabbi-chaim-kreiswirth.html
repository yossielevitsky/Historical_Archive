Rabbi Chaim Kreiswirth

הרב חיים קרייזווירט

Overview: Rabbi Chaim Kreiswirth was a leading Orthodox rabbinic authority and communal leader who served for nearly five decades as the Chief Rabbi of the Machzikei Hadass community in Antwerp, Belgium. He was widely regarded as one of the most gifted Torah scholars of his generation, known for his extraordinary breadth of knowledge, charismatic teaching style, and ability to inspire students and communities across the Jewish world.

Early Life and Education

Rabbi Kreiswirth was born in Galicia and studied in several of the major yeshivot of pre-war Eastern Europe. He attended the renowned Yeshivas Chachmei Lublin, founded by Rabbi Meir Shapiro. The institution was known for its elite admissions standards, requiring students to demonstrate extensive mastery of Talmud and classical Jewish texts even at a young age.

He later studied in the Slabodka Yeshiva, one of the central institutions of the Lithuanian mussar tradition. There, he absorbed the Lithuanian style of Torah study and developed relationships with leading rabbinic figures. During this period, he married the daughter of Rabbi Avraham Grodzinski, a prominent Slabodka figure connected to major rabbinic families, including Rabbi Yisrael Salanter and Rabbi Chaim Ozer Grodzinski. Through this marriage, he also became closely connected to Rabbi Yaakov Kamenetsky, who had a lasting influence on his life.

World War II and Refuge

During World War II, Rabbi Kreiswirth and his wife escaped from Nazi-occupied Europe. They eventually reached Mandatory Palestine, joining a group of European refugee scholars who found support and refuge among leading rabbinic authorities, including Rabbi Yitzchak Halevi Herzog, the Ashkenazi Chief Rabbi of Israel.

In the post-war years, he became recognized as an exceptional Torah scholar with remarkable memory and intellectual range. He was considered a gaon, capable of sustained, intensive Torah study across vast sections of Talmud and rabbinic literature.

Rabbinic Leadership in Chicago

In the late 1940s, Rabbi Kreiswirth was recommended by Rabbi Herzog for a teaching position in Chicago. Despite being relatively young, he was appointed as a rosh yeshiva.

His teaching style was highly distinctive. Rather than focusing narrowly on analytical step-by-step argumentation, his lectures emphasized breadth, integrating wide-ranging knowledge across Torah literature. His shiurim were intellectually demanding and often exceeded the level of preparation of many students, yet they were deeply inspiring and motivational. While not always accessible in full technical detail, his teachings had a strong impact on students, especially in strengthening commitment to Torah life and Jewish identity during the post-Holocaust rebuilding period.

Relationships and Educational Influence

Rabbi Kreiswirth maintained close relationships with leading rabbinic figures, including Rabbi Yaakov Yisrael Kanievsky (the Steipler Gaon) and Rabbi Avraham Yeshayahu Karelitz (the Chazon Ish). He was part of a broad network of European-trained rabbis who played a major role in rebuilding Jewish life after the war.

He was also deeply involved in supporting students beyond the classroom, often encouraging and assisting them in obtaining rabbinic and communal positions. Several of his students went on to serve in major leadership roles in Jewish communities worldwide.

Chief Rabbi of Antwerp

In 1955, Rabbi Kreiswirth was appointed Chief Rabbi of the Machzikei Hadass community in Antwerp, Belgium, a position he held for nearly five decades.

Originally, the Antwerp community had sought to appoint his uncle, Rabbi Yaakov Kamenetsky, who was at the time serving as Rosh Yeshiva of Torah Vodaath in New York. However, Rabbi Kamenetsky declined the invitation and was not interested in leaving his position. He instead recommended his nephew, Rabbi Chaim Kreiswirth, for the role.

Antwerp’s Orthodox Jewish community, largely composed of diamond merchants and businessmen, required strong rabbinic leadership capable of addressing complex halachic questions and communal disputes. Rabbi Kreiswirth became widely respected as a posek and communal authority. He was also actively involved in charitable work and in arranging marriages (shidduchim), particularly for individuals in need. His influence extended into both religious and social aspects of communal life.

Later Years and Medical Episode

During his later years, Rabbi Kreiswirth faced a serious medical diagnosis. He contacted the Steipler Gaon, Rabbi Yaakov Yisrael Kanievsky of Bnei Brak, and asked him to pray on his behalf. The Steipler responded by referencing a well-known beraisa:

“אֵלּוּ דְבָרִים שֶׁאָדָם אוֹכֵל פֵּרוֹתֵיהֶם בָּעוֹלָם הַזֶּה, וְהַקֶּרֶן קַיֶּמֶת לוֹ לָעוֹלָם הַבָּא”
(Eilu devarim she’adam ochel peiroteihen ba’olam hazeh, ve’hakeren kayemet lo la’olam haba)

This passage lists mitzvos whose principal reward is reserved for the World to Come, while also producing benefit in this world. Among them are bikur cholim (visiting the sick), levayas hameis (attending to the deceased), and hachnasas kallah (assisting in arranging marriages). The Steipler is reported to have explained that strengthening hachnasas kallah can serve as a merit that supports bikur cholim, thereby helping to avoid the need for levayas hameis.

Following this exchange, Rabbi Kreiswirth became increasingly involved in shidduchim, dedicating significant effort to assisting in the arrangement of marriages, especially for those in need.

Personality and Style

Rabbi Kreiswirth was known for his charisma, humor, and intellectual breadth. His public talks combined deep Torah scholarship with inspiration and encouragement. He often emphasized responsibility, action, and confidence in the future of the Jewish people. He maintained a broad outlook within the Orthodox world and supported students and initiatives across different segments of the community when he believed they contributed to Jewish continuity.

Legacy

Rabbi Kreiswirth passed away in 2001, after decades of leadership in Antwerp and beyond. He is remembered as a towering Torah scholar and communal leader who played a significant role in rebuilding Orthodox Jewish life in the post-Holocaust era. His legacy includes his scholarship, his students, and his impact on communities across Europe, Israel, and the United States. He is regarded as a figure who combined intellectual brilliance with warmth, leadership, and a deep commitment to the Jewish people.
