Rav Markus Mordechai Rottenberg

הרב מרדכי רוטנברג

Overview: Marcus (Mordechai) Rottenberg served as the Chief Rabbi of Antwerp's Orthodox community (Machsike Hadas) during the interwar years, guiding the community through rapid growth and the challenges of the Nazi occupation.

Leadership and Impact

Born in Poland, Rabbi Marcus Rottenberg was appointed Chief Rabbi of the Orthodox congregation Machsike Hadas in Antwerp in 1912. Under his leadership, the Orthodox community grew significantly, establishing new synagogues, schools, and charitable institutions. He was highly respected for his scholarship, wisdom, and dedication to maintaining traditional Jewish observance in a rapidly changing modern world.

Wartime Fate

During the German occupation of Belgium in World War II, Rabbi Rottenberg refused to abandon his congregation. During the Antwerp Pogrom of April 1941, his home was attacked and vandalized by collaborators. Despite the growing danger, he continued to provide spiritual guidance and support to his community. In 1943, he was arrested by the Gestapo and transferred to the Vittel internment camp in France, and subsequently deported to the Auschwitz-Birkenau extermination camp, where he was murdered. His legacy remains a symbol of spiritual resistance and dedication.
